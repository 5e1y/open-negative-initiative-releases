Nothing in this version changes the rendering of a photo you have already adjusted. Contact sheets are new, so nothing existed to move; every other change is interface, speed or reliability.

Contact sheets

  • Make a contact sheet from a selection. Frames are laid six to a row in one scene-linear TIFF, which is imported like any other photograph — so one balance, one set of curves and one export cover the whole roll.
  • The automatic balance reads inside the frames only. A window is placed on each cell, well inside its edges, so the gaps and the film rebate never enter the measurement — which is what used to drag the whole sheet off colour.
  • The gaps export black. A sheet reads as a sheet instead of arriving on a white page.
  • Every step of the grind is named. A forty-frame sheet used to sit for ten seconds on a full progress bar with nothing to say — writing, adding and opening now each announce themselves.

Rolls

  • Group frames into a roll with ⌘G, and name it. The gallery cuts on rolls instead of capture days, so a film shot over a week stops being scattered across it.
  • Membership lives in the sidecar. Deleting the index or moving to another machine cannot lose a roll, and a paste can never move a photograph out of one.

Presets

  • Five film presets ship with the app — Azure, Cinestill 800T, Gold 200, Portra 400, Redscale. They are laid down once, then they are yours: rename them, retune them, or throw them away and they stay away.

Framing

  • A horizontal and a vertical mirror. Both act on the frame as you see it, so the axis means what it says whatever quarter turns are applied.
  • Rotation turns clockwise, which is the direction the button was already claiming.
  • An Apply button under the panel's reset. Committing a crop no longer means finding the gesture elsewhere.

The bar at the top

  • Three groups instead of one row. Framing and comparison, then editing, then the two that commit — read left to right rather than scanned.
  • Delete is red, reset is amber, new contact sheet is orange. A deliberate choice: the gestures you cannot casually undo are the ones that carry a colour.

Speed and reliability

  • The library is read off the main thread. This version rebuilds its index at first launch, which costs one reading of each original; it now happens behind a progress sheet instead of an empty window.
  • Each original is read once, not twice. The fingerprint and the capture date both wanted the same metadata and each asked for it — 12.93 ms per photograph instead of 25.19.
  • A disconnected disk no longer poisons the library. What cannot be read is skipped rather than recorded with invented dates, and plugging the drive back in repairs it with no gesture. Thumbnails stop being thrown away, and frames stop piling onto one impossible day.
  • The gallery draws only what is on screen. Landing on the six-hundredth frame of an import used to build six hundred cells; the count is now what a window holds.
  • Column count replaces tile size, six or twelve, and ⌘− / ⌘+ walk it.

Smaller things

  • Conversion mode applies to a whole selection, as does reset.
  • Clicking a tab slides the panel instead of cutting to it, so the swipe the panel already supported is discoverable without being told about it.
