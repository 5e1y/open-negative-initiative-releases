Your photographs render exactly as before. No stage changed shape and no setting changed value: a conversion already made comes back identical, pixel for pixel. This version is about the memory the app holds while you work.

Scans no longer pile up in memory

Decoding a linear DNG built its pixels in a buffer the system's allocator then kept resident even after the app released it. Opening one photograph after another climbed by more than a gigabyte each time and never came back down. Those pages are now returned the moment a photograph is closed.

Measured over four consecutive opens of a 78-megapixel scan: what stays held afterwards went from 6.5 GB to 24 MB. Browsing a roll of scans now sits under a gigabyte where it used to reach four.

One less render at every open

Every photograph opened ran a full-frame measurement whose result nothing read. It is gone. The full-resolution frame is still decoded when a photograph opens — it has to be, for the loupe and for 100 % zoom — but off the main thread, so the window never waits on it.

A warning that could fire on a healthy photograph

The "flat decode" remark read an empty render as a flat frame. A buffer that comes back exactly zero everywhere is a failed measurement, not a photograph with no contrast, and it no longer raises the notice.

Under the hood

Nothing measured this app's memory before, which is why the leak of the previous version reached a user before it reached us. Four instruments now do, and they run from the command line: one drives thousands of slider writes, one opens photograph after photograph, one builds the state a step at a time, and one sweeps a slider across its whole travel counting the frames actually presented. Each reports the footprint alongside what Metal has allocated.

That last one found something the others could not: a probe that launches the app without a window renders nothing at all, and had been reporting comfortable numbers for a canvas that never drew a pixel. Work on drag responsiveness continues with an instrument that can now see it.
