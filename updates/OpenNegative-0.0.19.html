Your photographs render exactly as before. The new stage added here is skipped, bit for bit, until you move one of its points, so a conversion already made comes back identical. Nothing in this version needs a migration and nothing is lost by going back.

One habit does change on the first click. A photograph you have already worked on now opens on the Color panel instead of the framing view. A scan you have never touched still opens on framing, which is what a fresh negative wants.

Spectrogram finishing: the colour mixer, as a wheel

Photoshop and Lightroom put a colour mixer behind rows of sliders, where reading which colour you are steering means reading a label. It is the same control here, drawn as the thing it describes: eight points on a wheel, one per colour range, at the top of the Grade panel.

Each point carries three things. Its angle is that colour's hue, its distance from the centre its saturation, and a second point riding the same spoke its luminance. Click a point, then use the arrow keys — left and right turn the hue, up and down steer whichever point you are holding. Tab walks the wheel clockwise, Option-Tab the other way. Four keys under the graph do the same for a hand that would rather not leave the mouse, and a slider sets how far one press moves.

Double-clicking a point puts its own axes back to rest, and only its own: clearing a hue does not quietly undo the luminance the other point on that spoke is carrying.

What the wheel promises is that a band reaches its own colours and no others. Pushing red to the end of its travel leaves a blue pixel where it was, exactly — not nearly. The luminance point is a gain in stops, so a colour can be taken four times down without ever reaching black, and the hue and the saturation ratio come through it untouched.

Lighting, on black and white

The block offered two tabs, RGB and Luma. On a monochrome render that distinction has already collapsed — everything becomes one channel at the end of the chain — so the two were the same control under two different laws, one of them labelled RGB on a grey image, and their effects added up. Monochrome now offers the one set, and the tab bar goes away with the choice.

The Exposure handle also sat two thirds along its track while reading its resting value, on black and white only, which reads as already pushed. It rests at mid-travel now, in every mode. Only the handle moved: the stored value and the rendering did not.

Framing has a button

Cropping and straightening was a panel among others. It is now a key at the head of the toolbar, and while you are framing that key turns green and carries a check: press it and you are back where you were working, or on Color if you had not been anywhere yet. ⌘K does the same, and the gesture is in the View menu like every other one the bar carries.

The zoom follows you between photographs

Opening the next frame refitted the view, so walking a roll at 100 % to compare grain from one frame to the next was not possible. The zoom is now kept, and so is the fitted state if that is what you were on; only the pan is dropped, since it describes a picture you have left. The blurred stand-in shown while the next frame decodes is drawn at that same zoom instead of flashing back to fitted.

What it runs on

macOS 13 Ventura or newer, on Apple Silicon or Intel — one universal build for both.
